# M08v0.0.1 - Simple Dirichlet-multinomial

This model estimates support on the future election date at the coarsest
geographical scale that describes the colleges of both chambers.

Past Camera election results and polls are modelled as Dirichlet–multinomial
draws with uncertainty that increases with the time between the source and
the future election. In a joint election, Senato results borrow from the
realized Camera results according to how closely their filed lists match.

## Data

| Symbol | Definition and domain |
| --- | --- |
| $P$ | Stable political components required by the selected history and scenario; $K=\lvert P\rvert\ge1$ and $D=K+1$. |
| $0$ | Combined abstention plus blank/invalid voting, measured among electors. |
| $I$ | Historical elections, polls and reference pseudo-polls admitted by the information cutoff. |
| $t_i$ | Observation date of source $i$, in years. |
| $t_{\mathrm{next}}$ | Date of the election to be simulated, in years. |
| $U_i$ | For $i \in I$, geographical units for which the results of source $i$ are reported (commonly, municipalities at the time of election for elections; a single geographical scope for polls) |
| $X\in\{\mathrm C,\mathrm S\}$ | Camera or Senato, respectively. |
| $U^X$ | Lowest-level colleges for chamber $X$ in the future election: uninominal under Rosatellum, plurinominal or special uninominal under the 2026 reform. |
| $Q$ | Simulation cells, the coarsest common refinement of $U^{\mathrm C}$ and $U^{\mathrm S}$. |
| $C$ | Physical cells, the coarsest common refinement of $Q$ and the observed partitions $U_i$ within their scope. |
| $u_i(c)$ | Geographical unit from $U_i$ that contains physical cell $c \in C$ |
| $q(c)$ | Simulation cell from $Q$ that contains physical cell $c \in C$ |
| $E_{c,i}$ | Nonnegative integer electorate of cell $c$ at $t_i$; this is not a poll sample size. Each source has positive total electorate. |
| $E_c^X$ | Nonnegative integer electorate of physical cell $c$ for future chamber $X$. |
| $L_{u,i}$, $L_{c,i}$ | Political lists or answers offered by source $i$ in unit $u$ and physical cell $c$, respectively. $L_{u,i}$ is the union over cells in $u$. |
| $M_{c,i}$ | Binary list/answer by component membership matrix. Each column sums to zero or one; every political answer represents a nonempty component set. |
| $A_{c,i}$, $G_{\ell,c,i}$ | Offered components and the members of political list or answer $\ell$, derived from $M_{c,i}$. |
| $\boldsymbol y_{u,i}$ | Nonnegative integer election counts observed in unit $u$ from source $i$. |
| $\boldsymbol r_i$ | Complete, normalized reported composition, with entries in $[0,1]$. Retain the original published values and normalization provenance. |
| $n_i$, $\boldsymbol z_i$ | Positive integer respondent base and observed or reconstructed answer counts, with $\sum_\ell z_{\ell,i}=n_i$. References have $n_i=1000$. |

The nonempty component sets of the political lists partition $A_{c,i}$.

$$
\bigcup_{\ell\in L_{c,i}}G_{\ell,c,i}=A_{c,i},
\qquad G_{\ell,c,i}\cap G_{m,c,i}=\varnothing\quad(\ell\ne m).
$$

Define $G_{0,c,i}=\{0\}$ and include outcome $0$ in probability and count
vectors. For a political list absent from $c$, set $G_{\ell,c,i}=\varnothing$.
A declared Other answer represents the exact offered complement.
An absent component is not an observed zero.

Use a nonoverlapping geographic partition for each source.


### Reference values

Optional, per-list scenario-provided reference shares should be treated as a poll done at the provided reference date.

Reference shares sum must be lower than or equal to 1. If the sum is lower than 1, 
the remaining fraction should be assigned to an implicit "other" answer,
representing all the components not yet represented by lists with a reference share.

## Parameters

| Symbol | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| $\eta_{j}$ | For each $j \in \{0\} \cup P $, latent log national support at the future election date  |
| $\delta_{j, q}$ | For each $j \in \{0\} \cup P $ and $q \in Q$, difference between latent log local support and latent log national support  |
| $k_{\mathrm{election}}>0$ | Concentration for a standalone election at the forecast date. |
| $k_{\mathrm{poll}}>0$ | Concentration for a poll at the forecast date. |
| $\kappa_{\mathrm{link}}>k_{\mathrm{election}}$ | Concentration of Senato results conditional on Camera results when political ballots fully match. |
| $\tau>0$ | Time scale, in years, for the change in uncertainty with the observation lag. |


## Model


For each $q\in Q$ and component $j\in\{0\}\cup P$, define log support by

$$
s_{j,q} = \eta_{j} + \delta_{j,q}
$$

For each observed source $i$ and cell $c$ in its scope, restrict support to
the components offered there, including outcome $0$.

$$
\pi_{j,c,i} = \frac{e^{s_{j,q(c)}}}{\sum_{h \in \{0\} \cup A_{c,i}} e^{s_{h,q(c)}}}
$$

For a unit $u\in U_i$ with $N_{u,i}=\sum_{c\subseteq u}E_{c,i}>0$, define the
probability of outcome $\ell\in\{0\}\cup L_{u,i}$ by

$$
p_{\ell,u,i} =
\frac{\sum_{c\subseteq u} E_{c,i}\sum_{j\in G_{\ell,c,i}}\pi_{j,c,i}}{N_{u,i}}.
$$

Write $\operatorname{DM}(N,\boldsymbol\alpha)$ for a Dirichlet–multinomial
with total count $N$ and Dirichlet parameters $\boldsymbol\alpha$.
Categories with probability zero have deterministic zero counts, and a
unit with electorate zero has an all-zero election count vector. Let
$\Delta_i=t_{\mathrm{next}}-t_i$ be the observation lag. The historical
Camera likelihood is

$$
\boldsymbol y_{u,i}\sim\operatorname{DM}\!\left(
N_{u,i},\frac{k_{\mathrm{election}}}{1+\Delta_i/\tau}\boldsymbol p_{u,i}
\right).
$$

For a poll or reference pseudo-poll, let $u$ be the question's geographic scope.

$$
\boldsymbol z_i\sim\operatorname{DM}\!\left(
n_i,\frac{k_{\mathrm{poll}}}{1+\Delta_i/\tau}\boldsymbol p_{u,i}
\right).
$$

## Generation

### Chamber baselines and the Camera draw

For each chamber $X$ and cell $q$, let $A_q^X$ be the offered political
components and $G_{\ell,q}^X$ the component set of list $\ell$. Filings are
constant within each lowest-level college in each chamber. Define
$G_{0,q}^X=\{0\}$ and let $N_q^X=\sum_{c:q(c)=q}E_c^X$. The future baseline
uses the same ballot restriction as the observed sources.

$$
\pi_{j,q}^X=\frac{e^{s_{j,q}}}{\sum_{h\in\{0\}\cup A_q^X}e^{s_{h,q}}},
\qquad
p_{\ell,q}^X=\sum_{j\in G_{\ell,q}^X}\pi_{j,q}^X.
$$

For each Camera college $u\in U^{\mathrm C}$ with positive electorate, draw
its count vector from

$$
N_u^{\mathrm C}=\sum_{q\subseteq u}N_q^{\mathrm C},
\qquad
\boldsymbol p_u^{\mathrm C}
=\frac{1}{N_u^{\mathrm C}}\sum_{q\subseteq u}N_q^{\mathrm C}\boldsymbol p_q^{\mathrm C},
\qquad
\boldsymbol Y_u^{\mathrm C}\sim\operatorname{DM}\!\left(
N_u^{\mathrm C},k_{\mathrm{election}}\boldsymbol p_u^{\mathrm C}
\right).
$$

If $u$ crosses Senato college boundaries, allocate its realized counts to
its simulation cells by raking the baseline table
$N_q^{\mathrm C}p_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}$. The estimated cell counts
$x_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}$ may be fractional and satisfy

$$
\sum_\ell x_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}=N_q^{\mathrm C},
\qquad
\sum_{q\subseteq u}x_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}=Y_{\ell,u}^{\mathrm C}.
$$

If $u$ consists of one simulation cell, use the college counts directly.
Set the counts to zero in cells with zero Camera electorate. Smooth the
realized shares with a fixed $\epsilon=1$ pseudo-elector so that a filed
list with a realized Camera count of zero can still receive Senato votes.

$$
\widehat p_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}
=\frac{x_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}+\epsilon p_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm C}}
       {N_q^{\mathrm C}+\epsilon}.
$$

### Transferring the Camera result to the Senato ballot

Let $m_X(j,q)$ be the list containing offered component $j$ in chamber $X$,
with $m_X(0,q)=0$, and write $G_q^X(j)=G_{m_X(j,q),q}^X$. For each
$j\in\{0\}\cup A_q^{\mathrm S}$, take the realized Camera list's ratio to
baseline where the component is shared. Apply those ratios to the Senato
baseline and regroup onto the Senato ballot.

$$
d_{j,q}=
\begin{cases}
\widehat p_{m_{\mathrm C}(j,q),q}^{\mathrm C}/
p_{m_{\mathrm C}(j,q),q}^{\mathrm C}, & j\in\{0\}\cup A_q^{\mathrm C},\\
1, & j\in A_q^{\mathrm S}\setminus A_q^{\mathrm C},
\end{cases}
\qquad
b_{\ell,q}
=\frac{\sum_{j\in G_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm S}}\pi_{j,q}^{\mathrm S}d_{j,q}}
       {\sum_{j\in\{0\}\cup A_q^{\mathrm S}}\pi_{j,q}^{\mathrm S}d_{j,q}}.
$$

All components of a grouped Camera list receive the same multiplier.
When that list splits in Senato, the model's baseline component shares
determine the relative support of those components.

For a set of political components $B$, define $h_q(B)=\sum_{j\in B}e^{s_{j,q}}$.
Weight the transfer by how much political support the two ballots share
and how similarly each shared component is grouped.

$$
w_q =
\frac{
  \displaystyle\sum_{j\in A_q^{\mathrm C}\cap A_q^{\mathrm S}}
  e^{s_{j,q}}
  \frac{h_q(G_q^{\mathrm C}(j)\cap G_q^{\mathrm S}(j))}
       {h_q(G_q^{\mathrm C}(j)\cup G_q^{\mathrm S}(j))}
}{h_q(A_q^{\mathrm C}\cup A_q^{\mathrm S})}.
$$

Use $w_q=0$ if $N_q^{\mathrm C}=0$ or the political union is empty.
Otherwise identical partitions of offered components give $w_q=1$,
disjoint offered components give $w_q=0$, and partial overlap, splits and
mergers give $0<w_q<1$. Outcome $0$ participates in the transfer through
$b_{\ell,q}$. The weight depends only on the political filings and their
baseline support.

### The Senato draw

For each Senato college $v\in U^{\mathrm S}$ with positive electorate
$N_v^{\mathrm S}=\sum_{q\subseteq v}N_q^{\mathrm S}$, set
$a_{q,v}=N_q^{\mathrm S}/N_v^{\mathrm S}$. Given the collection of Camera
college draws $\boldsymbol Y^{\mathrm C}$, use the following mean and
concentration for a single Senato college draw.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\ell,v}
  &=\sum_{q\subseteq v}a_{q,v}
    \left[(1-w_q)p_{\ell,q}^{\mathrm S}+w_q b_{\ell,q}\right],
&\quad \bar w_v&=\sum_{q\subseteq v}a_{q,v}w_q,\\
\rho_v
  &=\frac{1-\bar w_v}{k_{\mathrm{election}}+1}
    +\frac{\bar w_v}{\kappa_{\mathrm{link}}+1},
& \kappa_v&=\rho_v^{-1}-1,\\
\boldsymbol Y_v^{\mathrm S}\mid\boldsymbol Y^{\mathrm C},s
  &\sim \operatorname{DM}\!\left(N_v^{\mathrm S},\kappa_v\boldsymbol\mu_v\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

The scale $\rho=1/(\kappa+1)$ interpolates the extra-multinomial variance
at a fixed mean. With full matching, the mean is the smoothed Camera
realization reweighted by Senato cell electorates and the concentration
is $\kappa_{\mathrm{link}}$. With disjoint ballots the mean is the Senato
baseline and the concentration is $k_{\mathrm{election}}$, giving a fresh
same-day election draw conditional on the shared $s$.

## Priors and fitting

Take the indications about priors as a rule-of-thumb guidance for setting prior parameters, they not need to be calculated for every scenario and they
can be adapted if a good fitting needs more strict or relaxed prior
distributions.

| Parameter | Prior |
| --- | --- |
| $\eta_{j}$ | For each $j\in\{0\}\cup P$, weakly informative priors, centered so that in a hypothetical election with all components represented and all $\delta_{j,q}=0$, the expected value of $\pi_{0,c,i}$ is $0.5$ and that of each political component $\pi_{j,c,i}$ is $0.5/K$. Uncertainty should be set to let $\pi_{j,c,i}$ values below $\frac{E_{min}}{\sum_{c \in C} E_{c,i}}$ or above $\frac{(\sum_{c \in C} E_{c,i})-E_{min}}{\sum_{c \in C} E_{c,i}}$ be highly improbable for countrywide elections, where $E_{min}$ is the minimum number of signatures normally required to present a list not yet present in parliament. |
| $\delta_{j,q}$ | For each $j\in\{0\}\cup P$ and $q \in Q$, the same weakly informative prior, centered to zero. Uncertainty should be set to make improbable local $\pi_{j,c,i}$ more than 10 times greater or smaller than mean national share. |
| $k_{\mathrm{election}}$ | Informative prior that makes differences of more than five percentage points highly improbable between standalone elections at $\Delta_i=0$, all else equal, for shares around 5–25%. |
| $k_{\mathrm{poll}}$ | Informative prior that makes differences of more than five percentage points improbable between a poll and an election at $\Delta_i=0$, all else equal, for shares around 5–25%. |
| $\kappa_{\mathrm{link}}$ | Use a prior favoring small same-day differences for fully matched ballots. Estimate the concentration from paired returns. |
| $\tau$ | Informative prior, set to make share variations of 2 times greater or smaller improbable between elections one year apart. |

### Calibrating the chamber link

Fit $\kappa_{\mathrm{link}}$ with the conditional Senato likelihood using
same-date paired returns on comparable electorate and geography at the
modelled college scale. Use the observed Camera counts on the shared
geography when available. Enter the paired Camera counts through the
Camera marginal likelihood and the paired Senato counts through the
conditional likelihood for the same election. Use the same-day
concentrations for the within-election link.

The [comparable 18+ pair in the bundled data](../../research/vote-generation/campaign-2026-11/results.md#proposed-chamber-criterion-and-results)
is 2022. It informs differences across colleges within one election.
Check dispersion and signed list-level gaps. With fully matched ballots
the conditional mean assumes zero signed gap against the smoothed
Camera shares on the same electorate weighting.

For Monte Carlo fitting, choose initial values that support convergence.
