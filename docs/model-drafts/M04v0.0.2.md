# M04v0.0.2 Hierarchical log-ratio drift and Dirichlet model

Status: research specification. This is the current M04 draft; version 0.0.1
is retained unchanged for comparison. No fitting framework, production
implementation, predictive acceptance or browser performance is established.

The model separates baseline support, persistent geography, temporal movement,
European-election differences, national source error and local source variation.
Elections use integer vote counts. Polls and scenario references use
Dirichlet-multinomial counts, including observed shares equal to zero or one.

## Data and admissible inputs

| Symbol | Definition and domain |
| --- | --- |
| $P$ | Stable political components required by the selected history and scenario; $K=\lvert P\rvert\ge1$ and $D=K+1$. |
| $0$ | Combined abstention plus blank/invalid voting, measured among electors. |
| $I$ | Historical elections, polls and reference pseudo-polls admitted by the information cutoff. Camera and Senato are distinct sources; exclude age-25+ Senato elections. |
| $t_i$, $T$ | Observation date of source $i$ and the set of distinct admitted dates. |
| $U$ | Fixed statistical geographic units owning latent local support. See [geography](#historical-geography-and-physical-cells). |
| $C$, $u(c)$ | Physical ballot cells and the statistical unit containing cell $c$. |
| $C_i$ | Cells partitioning the source's target territory, including for polls. Every elector is represented once within this partition. |
| $E_{c,i}$ | Nonnegative integer electorate of cell $c$ at $t_i$; this is not a poll sample size. Each source has positive total electorate. |
| $L_{c,i}$ | Political lists or question answers offered in cell $c$. |
| $M_{c,i}$ | Binary list/answer by component membership matrix. Each column sums to zero or one; every political answer represents a nonempty component set. |
| $A_{c,i}$, $G_{l,c,i}$ | Offered components and the members of political list/answer $l$, derived from $M_{c,i}$. |
| $y_{l,c,i}$ | Observed election counts where available at cell resolution; nonnegative integers. Preserve coarser original observations instead of fabricating cell counts. |
| $\boldsymbol r_i$ | Complete, normalized reported composition, with entries in $[0,1]$. Retain the original published values and normalization provenance. |
| $n_i$, $\boldsymbol z_i$ | Positive integer respondent base and observed/reconstructed answer counts, with $\sum_l z_{l,i}=n_i$. References have $n_i=1000$. |
| $d_i$ | One for European elections or European-election intention questions; zero for political elections/questions. Classification follows the election being asked about. |

For political lists, the nonempty sets $G_{l,c,i}$ partition $A_{c,i}$:

$$
\bigcup_{l\in L_{c,i}}G_{l,c,i}=A_{c,i},
\qquad G_{l,c,i}\cap G_{m,c,i}=\varnothing\quad(l\ne m).
$$

Define separately $G_{0,c,i}=\{0\}$. Outcome $0$ is always available.
For a poll or reference question, $L_{c,i}$ and $M_{c,i}$ are constant over
$C_i$. A declared Other answer represents the exact offered complement.
An absent component is not an observed zero.

Use a nonoverlapping geographic partition for each source. If a catalogue
contains both Camera and Senato cells, select the relevant partition or a common
refinement; never sum both representations of the same electorate for a poll.

## Support and source variation

### Baseline, scales and identification

Let $\boldsymbol q_t$ be a strictly positive simplex vector and let
$\kappa>0$ be a finite concentration, constant over time in this version:

$$
\boldsymbol\alpha_t=\kappa\boldsymbol q_t,
\qquad \sum_jq_{j,t}=1,
\qquad \sum_j\alpha_{j,t}=\kappa.
$$

The local effects $\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t}$ are signed log support multipliers,
not additive concentrations. Use the score space
$\mathcal H=\{\boldsymbol x\in\mathbb R^D:\sum_jx_j=0\}$ for local effects,
European effects and source errors. $\mathcal N_{\mathcal H}(0,s^2I)$ means
isotropic Gaussian coordinates in an orthonormal basis of this space.

Fix versioned reference-electorate weights $w_u\ge0$ with $\sum_uw_u=1$ and
impose $\sum_uw_u\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t}=0$. For a collection of score vectors,
define the geographic centering operator

$$
(\mathcal L\boldsymbol x)_u
=\boldsymbol x_u-\sum_vw_v\boldsymbol x_v.
$$

Apply this operator to terminal local draws and local temporal innovations.
Local vectors are independent before this projection, not afterwards.
The constraints separate national baseline scores from common local shifts.

$\boldsymbol q_t$ is a national baseline on the log-ratio scale. Actual national
shares are geographic aggregates after local effects and ballot conditioning;
they need not equal $\boldsymbol q_t$.

### European effect and national source error

Let $\boldsymbol\gamma\in\mathcal H$ be a time-constant European-election
effect, including an effect on non-valid support. There is no systematic
Camera/Senato difference in this version.

Each recorded source has an independent national error, conditional on its scale:

$$
\boldsymbol\epsilon_i
\sim\mathcal N_{\mathcal H}
(\boldsymbol0,\sigma_{\mathrm{source}}^2I).
$$

Its realization is shared across that source's entire territory. Camera and
Senato held on the same day share temporal states but have distinct source errors
and local source realizations. Correlations between different surveys, house
effects and additional source-type error scales are later improvements.

### Local source realization and ballot probabilities

For each statistical unit intersecting $C_i$:

$$
\mu_{j,u,i}=
\frac{q_{j,t_i}\exp(\delta_{j,u,t_i}+d_i\gamma_j+\epsilon_{j,i})}
{\sum_{h\in\{0\}\cup P}
q_{h,t_i}\exp(\delta_{h,u,t_i}+d_i\gamma_h+\epsilon_{h,i})},
$$

$$
\boldsymbol\pi_{u,i}
\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(\kappa\boldsymbol\mu_{u,i}).
$$

These draws are independent between units conditional on the shared states and
source error. Every physical part of the same unit uses the same
$\boldsymbol\pi_{u,i}$ for this source. For $l\in\{0\}\cup L_{c,i}$:

$$
p_{l,c,i}=
\frac{\sum_{j\in G_{l,c,i}}\pi_{j,u(c),i}}
{\pi_{0,u(c),i}+\sum_{h\in A_{c,i}}\pi_{h,u(c),i}}.
$$

All concentrations remain positive. Ballot-unavailable components are removed
before count generation; no votes are generated and relocated afterwards.

## Time and drift

Use elapsed calendar days divided by $365.25$ as the time unit. Election dates
are election days; poll dates are fieldwork midpoints when known, otherwise the
recorded observation dates with that convention retained. Reference dates are
explicit. Availability/publication dates separately determine cutoff eligibility.

Let $t^\star=\max T$ be the terminal fitting anchor, not the future election date.
For centered national log support, define

$$
\boldsymbol a_t=\log\boldsymbol q_t
-\frac1D\left(\sum_j\log q_{j,t}\right)\boldsymbol1,
\qquad \boldsymbol q_t=\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol a_t).
$$

For consecutive admitted dates $t_1<t_2$, let $\Delta=t_2-t_1$ in years.
Starting at the terminal prior and generating backwards:

$$
\boldsymbol a_{t_1}=\boldsymbol a_{t_2}
+\sigma_\alpha\sqrt\Delta\,\boldsymbol\xi_{t_1,t_2},
\qquad \boldsymbol\xi_{t_1,t_2}\sim\mathcal N_{\mathcal H}(0,I),
$$

$$
\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t_1}=\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t_2}
+\sigma_\delta\sqrt\Delta\,
(\mathcal L\boldsymbol\zeta_{t_1,t_2})_u,
\qquad \boldsymbol\zeta_{u,t_1,t_2}\sim\mathcal N_{\mathcal H}(0,I).
$$

Innovations are independent between intervals, and between the national and local
processes; local innovations are independent between units before projection.
The scales are nonnegative, measured in log support per square root of a year.
The previous $\lambda_\alpha,\lambda_\delta$ parameters are removed.

Future states use an explicitly defined forward branch from the fitted terminal
state. For $h>0$ in years:

$$
\boldsymbol a_{t^\star+h}=\boldsymbol a_{t^\star}
+\sigma_\alpha\sqrt h\,\boldsymbol\xi^+,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t^\star+h}=\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t^\star}
+\sigma_\delta\sqrt h\,(\mathcal L\boldsymbol\zeta^+)_u.
$$

Future innovations are independent of historical innovations conditional on the
terminal states and scales. Multiple future dates use a shared path with
independent interval increments. Equal dates use the same state with no temporal
innovation; new elections still receive new source realizations.

Gaussian transitions compose exactly when unobserved dates are inserted. This
is zero drift in log ratios, not exact preservation of expected arithmetic shares.
Check the induced mean movement and long-horizon tails during evaluation.
No prior is added independently at earlier dates. Moving the anchor changes the
root-prior/fit identity and requires a new fit from eligible evidence; a previous
posterior may initialize computation but is not reused as new evidence.

## Election observations

Retain legally attributed list counts, including legal allocation of
candidate-only votes before fitting. For an observed physical cell:

$$
V_{c,i}=\sum_{l\in L_{c,i}}y_{l,c,i},
\qquad y_{0,c,i}=E_{c,i}-V_{c,i},
$$

$$
\left(y_{0,c,i},(y_{l,c,i})_{l\in L_{c,i}}\right)
\mid\boldsymbol p_{c,i}
\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(E_{c,i},\boldsymbol p_{c,i}).
$$

Reject inconsistent counts or negative $y_0$. Future generation uses the same
conditional count model and conserves every cell's electorate.

For one unit with a common ballot, the local Dirichlet draw can be integrated out.
With $\beta_{j,u,i}=\kappa\mu_{j,u,i}$, define

$$
b_{0,c,i}=\beta_{0,u(c),i},
\qquad b_{l,c,i}=\sum_{j\in G_{l,c,i}}\beta_{j,u(c),i}.
$$

The resulting counts have a Dirichlet-multinomial distribution with concentration
vector $\boldsymbol b_{c,i}$. Cells sharing a local realization remain dependent
after integration. Identical-ballot cells can be aggregated before this operation;
different ballots or different local realizations generally require their joint
aggregate likelihood. Do not multiply independent collapsed cell likelihoods
when those cells share $\boldsymbol\pi_{u,i}$.

## Poll and reference observations

### Geographic aggregation and denominators

Over the target territory $C_i$, define

$$
Q_{l,i}=\frac{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}p_{l,c,i}}
{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}},
\qquad
Q_{0,i}=\frac{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}p_{0,c,i}}
{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}}.
$$

For political shares among valid voters:

$$
R_{l,i}=\frac{Q_{l,i}}{1-Q_{0,i}}
=\frac{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}p_{l,c,i}}
{\sum_{c\in C_i}E_{c,i}(1-p_{0,c,i})}.
$$

These equations use territory electorates, never respondent counts, as geographic
weights. The observation model assumes the question estimates this target
population; incompatible population/denominator definitions require an explicit
conversion assumption or exclusion reason.

### Respondent bases and count construction

For a poll, $n_i$ is its recorded positive integer number of respondents for the
modeled question and denominator. Use the decided political-answer respondent
base for a composition conditional on valid political answers, and the all-answer
base for a question including non-valid responses. Do not use contacted persons,
electorates or a design-effect-adjusted effective sample in this version.
If only an overall survey respondent count is known, using it as the question
base requires a declared approximation. A missing usable base requires an
explicit positive integer base assumption or an exclusion reason.

For a reference pseudo-poll, set $n_i=1000$, irrespective of geography or any
scenario-provided reference uncertainty. This is declared pseudo-evidence
strength, not a measured respondent count or an exact constraint.

Use actual integer answer counts when available for the same question base.
Otherwise reconstruct a count composition deterministically:

1. Require a complete, normalized $\boldsymbol r_i$ and set
   $f_l=n_ir_{l,i}$, $z_l=\lfloor f_l\rfloor$.
2. Allocate the remaining $n_i-\sum_lz_l$ units to the largest fractional
   remainders, breaking ties by stable choice ID order.
3. Preserve raw shares, base, method and a reconstructed-count flag. Record any
   normalization of published shares upstream; do not silently invent missing
   answers or normalize an inconsistent reference.

This largest-remainder reconstruction conserves $n_i$ and retains exact zero
counts. It is an approximation for weighted or rounded percentages, not recovered
individual answers or a likelihood for publication rounding. Positive shares
below the count resolution may become zero; retain the original values and
report precision loss. References have a $0.001$ share grid (0.1 percentage point).
Do not add pseudocounts or force every positive printed share to receive a count.

### Count likelihoods and exact boundary shares

For political-only questions, use

$$
\boldsymbol\theta_i\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(k\boldsymbol R_i),
\qquad
\boldsymbol z_i\mid\boldsymbol\theta_i
\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n_i,\boldsymbol\theta_i),
$$

or integrate $\boldsymbol\theta_i$ exactly:

$$
\boldsymbol z_i\sim
\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}(n_i,k\boldsymbol R_i).
$$

Here $k>0$ is a common finite overdispersion concentration, distinct from $\kappa$.
The extra answer-count layer applies to polls and references. Local geographic
source variation and the national source error remain separate parts of the model.

For a non-valid-only question, use its non-valid count:

$$
z_{0,i}\sim\operatorname{BetaBinomial}
\left(n_i,kQ_{0,i},k(1-Q_{0,i})\right).
$$

A joint all-elector question instead uses
$\operatorname{DirichletMultinomial}(n_i,k(Q_{0,i},(Q_{l,i})_l))$.
Use its joint counts once; do not also enter the derived non-valid and
conditional political shares as independent observations. A political-only
question uses $k\boldsymbol R_i$ as specified above; the conditional political
distribution induced by a joint question has its own implied concentration.

**Decision for 0.0.2:** accept observed shares equal to zero or one through these
count likelihoods. A nonempty answer with $z_l=0$, or an answer receiving all
$n_i$ responses, has positive finite probability under positive concentrations.
It is finite-sample evidence, not a structural zero/one latent support constraint.
An answer representing no component is a different input issue: remove an empty
zero-mass residual with provenance, and reject positive mass on an empty group.
A one-answer question has probability one and supplies no compositional evidence.

Rejecting endpoint shares under a continuous Dirichlet likelihood would discard
valid finite-sample outcomes and explicit reference values. Clamping them to
arbitrary interior values changes the evidence. The count model avoids those
problems and makes sample size explicit. Reconstruction remains approximate for
printed/weighted shares; a publication-rounding likelihood is deferred.

The conditional answer-share variance is

$$
\operatorname{Var}(z_{l,i}/n_i\mid\boldsymbol R_i,k)
=R_{l,i}(1-R_{l,i})\frac{n_i+k}{n_i(k+1)}.
$$

Thus $n_i$ and $k$ have distinct roles; the multinomial-equivalent sample size
is $n_i(k+1)/(n_i+k)$. For a reference with $n_i=1000$ and $k=1600$, this is about
616, giving a conditional 50%-share standard deviation of about 2.01 percentage
points before the other model uncertainties. The count model therefore changes
the earlier continuous-share variance and requires fresh prior calibration.

### Geographic and non-valid scenario references

References are pseudo-polls at their supplied dates over their supplied scopes.
Build $C_i$ from that scope, retaining partial-cell weights and their provenance
where a geographic boundary requires refinement. Parts of one statistical unit
retain their shared support. Reference declarations do not create new local units.

Combine compatible rows into one question per date, scope, election context and
declared membership interpretation. Avoid duplicating a common reference merely
because the future election has two chambers. Memberships that differ between
chambers require explicitly distinct question interpretations.

Political reference shares sum to at most one. Assign a positive remainder to an
implicit Other answer containing all components not represented by the named
answers. Reject overlapping membership sets, shares outside $[0,1]$, sums above
one, and a positive remainder with an empty complement. A zero share for a
nonempty answer remains an observed zero count.

Use the declared reference question's pre-filing membership sets when measuring
support. Future ballot filing subsequently conditions generated probabilities;
it need not preserve the supplied reference as a post-filing territorial margin.
Per-reference SD/uncertainty fields are not consumed by this model version.
References use the same $k$ and source-error law as polls, with fixed $n_i=1000$.

A non-valid reference measures abstention plus blank/invalid voting as a fraction
of electors. A turnout-only percentage does not determine it without additional
information or an explicit conversion assumption.

If one reference statement supplies non-valid fraction $r_{0,i}$ and conditional
political shares $\boldsymbol r_i$, construct the joint composition

$$
\left(r_{0,i},(1-r_{0,i})\boldsymbol r_i\right)
$$

and apportion its 1000 pseudo-responses once. At $r_{0,i}=1$, this joint statement
contains no political-answer counts; political assertions intended as separate
evidence must be declared as a separate question.

## Priors

### Terminal baseline only

A proper terminal prior is required for permitted scenarios with unidentified or
unobserved components. Specify it on the simplex, not as an implicit flat prior
on unconstrained log scores. A sampler parameterized in log scores must include
the corresponding change-of-variables Jacobian:

$$
B\sim\operatorname{Beta}(2,2),
\qquad \boldsymbol s\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}(2\boldsymbol1_K),
\qquad B\perp\boldsymbol s,
$$

$$
q_{0,t^\star}=B,
\qquad q_{j,t^\star}=(1-B)s_j\quad(j\in P),
\qquad \boldsymbol\alpha_{t^\star}=\kappa\boldsymbol q_{t^\star}.
$$

This gives $\mathbb E[q_{0,t^\star}]=0.5$ and
$\mathbb E[q_{j,t^\star}]=0.5/K$. These expectations apply only to the terminal
baseline. There is no calibration intercept, constraint or required expectation
of 0.5 on $\mu_0$, $\pi_0$, geographic aggregates or other dates. Priors on local,
European and source effects may change their induced means through normalization.
For $K=1$, the political composition is the deterministic vector $(1)$.

### Scale and effect priors

All priors below are independent before the stated transformations. Normal
distributions are written with variance; $\operatorname{LogNormal}(m,s)$ means
that the natural logarithm is $\mathcal N(m,s^2)$. These numerical choices are
initial research settings, not fitted or accepted defaults.

| Parameter | Initial prior |
| --- | --- |
| National temporal scale $\sigma_\alpha$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log0.15,0.35)$ |
| Local temporal scale $\sigma_\delta$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log0.10,0.35)$ |
| Terminal local scale $\tau_{\mathrm{geo}}$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log0.60,0.25)$ |
| National source scale $\sigma_{\mathrm{source}}$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log0.04,0.30)$ |
| Local concentration $\kappa$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log2000,0.50)$ |
| Answer-count concentration $k$ | $\operatorname{LogNormal}(\log1600,0.50)$ |
| Raw European political effects $g_j$, $j\in P$ | Independent $\mathcal N(0,0.35^2)$ |
| Raw European non-valid effect $g_0$ | $\mathcal N(0,0.70^2)$ |

Set $\boldsymbol\gamma=\boldsymbol g-D^{-1}(\sum_jg_j)\boldsymbol1$.
For the terminal local prior, draw independent
$\boldsymbol v_u\sim\mathcal N_{\mathcal H}(0,\tau_{\mathrm{geo}}^2I)$ and set
$\boldsymbol\delta_{u,t^\star}=(\mathcal L\boldsymbol v)_u$.
No independent prior is added to each later or earlier local state.

Before accepting a prior configuration, evaluate probability-scale targets:

- Annual support changes beyond a factor of two, local support differing tenfold
  from the national baseline, and source or poll discrepancies above five
  percentage points should ordinarily have probability below 5% per assessed
  component. Specify the comparison, baseline shares and horizon in each check;
  relative bounds cannot hold uniformly for arbitrarily rare components.
- Following M03's elicitation idea, optionally declare and pin a positive
  $E_{\min}$ and evaluate terminal-baseline tails below
  $\varepsilon=E_{\min}/E_{\mathrm{total}}$ or above $1-\varepsilon$, initially
  targeting at most 1% probability per component. This is a model-elicitation
  count, not a legal minimum vote share or a hard support constraint.
- Include concentration uncertainty, the chosen component basis and respondent
  bases in checks. The new count likelihood adds sampling variance; do not reuse
  continuous-share calibration claims. Local tail checks include $\kappa$ as
  well as $\delta$.

Change and version priors only from declared development/calibration evidence.
Positive finite scales regularize otherwise unidentified directions; they do not
establish identification or predictive accuracy. Retain posterior uncertainty in
these scales during generation.

## Historical geography and physical cells

Use fixed statistical units supported by explicit historical crosswalks, normally
municipalities, and separate them from the physical ballot partition of each
source or future chamber. Resolve administrative merges/splits through exact
crosswalks or common coarsening of the affected units where finer reconstruction
is unavailable. Label the resulting resolution loss and any assumed common
profile; do not silently treat a merger family as individually observed children.

Keep original historical observations. If observed unit $H$ covers several
modeled cells, its count is the sum of their latent counts after compatible
observable list/group aggregation:

$$
y_{l,H,i}=\sum_{c\in H}Y_{l,c,i}.
$$

Use the induced aggregate likelihood, or an exact common-ballot/common-profile
reduction where applicable. Counts assigned to finer cells by estimated weights
are not independent observed counts. Estimated electorate allocations may be
needed to define the finer model; pin and label those assumptions and conserve
the authoritative observed electorate. Missing fine geography never becomes zero.

For future generation, allocate authoritative municipal electorates to physical
cells as nonnegative integers with an explicit, deterministic, versioned
apportionment rule, initially largest remainders with stable cell-ID ties. Pin
the population/crosswalk weights. Each cell uses its own filed choices and the
shared $\boldsymbol\pi_{u,i}$. Conditional counts are independent between cells;
their common support uncertainty is retained. Splitting identical-ballot cells
preserves the aggregate count distribution, not necessarily identical seeded
draws under a different cell inventory.

## Identification and research gates

Prior-controlled posterior allocation of unidentified components is an explicit
assumption. Grouped counts remain grouped observations. Where the likelihood is
invariant to internal component contrasts, report the affected group/scope as
prior-driven with warning severity; do not reject it solely for that reason or
claim that a finite posterior identifies its split. Carry shared national split
uncertainty across municipalities instead of averaging independent allocations
into artificial national certainty. Component resolution and prior sensitivity
belong in the reported model assumptions.

Before full-data fitting, use progressive synthetic fixtures covering grouped and
unobserved components, zero/full response counts, reconstructed counts, matched
and heterogeneous ballots, geographic aggregation, shared municipality parts,
same-day Camera/Senato sources and European effects. Check exact count conservation,
posterior reliability in weakly identified directions, probability-scale priors,
forecast mean movement, and computational growth.

Fitting framework selection, empirical prior tuning, predictive comparison and
browser feasibility remain subsequent tasks in [the migration plan](../../TODO.md).
Persist the component/geography basis, weights, evidence/cutoff dates, respondent
bases, reconstruction policy, priors and numerical/RNG identities for reproducible
research. Specification completion does not activate a production generator.

Later model improvements include regional effects, source-type scales,
survey design effects/effective samples, publication-rounding likelihoods,
shared survey errors and richer geography. Raw respondent sample size is already
part of this version; the other survey refinements remain deferred.
